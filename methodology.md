# Decision Lab Methodology

## Objective

The purpose of the Decision Lab is to compare common workforce management levers using the same operating environment.

The question is not whether each lever helps.

The question is which lever provides the greatest service-level impact relative to business effort.

## Scenarios Modeled

### 1. Baseline

Represents current operating performance using existing interval-level demand, staffing, AHT, and shrinkage assumptions.

### 2. AHT Reduction

Models the effect of reducing Average Handle Time.

This is a common leadership response because it appears controllable through coaching, process improvement, or technology. However, the impact may be limited if the primary service-level issue is schedule distribution.

### 3. Shrinkage Reduction

Models the effect of reducing shrinkage and increasing productive availability.

This may improve service level, but it can require policy changes, coaching, attendance management, or schedule adherence improvement.

### 4. Add Headcount

Models the effect of adding staff capacity.

This can improve service level but usually carries the highest direct cost.

### 5. Schedule Redistribution

Models the effect of moving existing staffing into the intervals where demand pressure is highest.

This scenario tests whether the organization already has enough total staffing but is misaligned at the interval level.

### 6. Combined Strategy

Models a broader intervention using multiple improvements together.

This can produce the strongest result but may be more difficult to execute.

## Analytical Logic

The model compares required staffing to available staffing at the interval level.

For each interval:

- Forecasted contact volume is converted into workload.
- AHT is applied to estimate workload demand.
- Staffing is adjusted for shrinkage.
- Service-level performance is estimated based on staffing sufficiency.
- Scenario changes are applied consistently across the same dataset.

## Interpretation

The Decision Lab is not intended to replace enterprise WFM systems.

It is intended to demonstrate decision logic:

- Which lever should leadership prioritize?
- Is the problem headcount volume or schedule distribution?
- Are expensive interventions being considered before lower-cost operational fixes?
- Does daily staffing hide interval-level failure?

## Key Insight

The largest modeled improvement came from schedule redistribution.

This indicates that the organization did not primarily have a staffing-volume problem.

It had a staffing-distribution problem.

## Business Use

This type of analysis can support:

- Budget discussions
- Staffing strategy
- Schedule design
- Intraday planning
- Operations leadership reviews
- WFM process improvement
- Capacity planning recommendations
